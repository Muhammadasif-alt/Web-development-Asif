Landscaping SEO Playbook

How to win landscaping and roofing work without shutting the door on e-commerce and real estate — the domain, the page structure, and the order to do the SEO work in.

Industries: landscaping · roofing · real estate · e-commerce Markets: US · UK · AU · CA · IE · PT Based: Pakistan

The rule everything else follows

Niche the message, never the domain

Landscapers and roofers are the right thing to chase — they have money, they need websites, and almost nobody good is competing for them. But you also build e-commerce stores, real-estate sites, and general business sites, and that work pays too.

Those two facts look like they conflict. They do not, as long as each one sits in the right place. The domain stays neutral; the pages get specific. A domain is permanent and rides on every email you send — the moment it says landscaper, a real-estate client quietly decides you are not for them. A page costs nothing and can be as narrow as you like.

Google ranks pages, not domains. A page about landscaping websites — landscaping photos, landscaping case studies, landscaping words throughout — ranks for landscaping searches whatever the domain says. That is where the entire niche advantage actually lives.

Correction to earlier advice

An earlier version of this page put industry names like landscaperleads.com at the top of the shortlist. That was written before e-commerce and real estate came up, and it is the wrong call for you. Those names lock the whole business into one trade. The neutral shortlist below replaces them.

Still true from before: no country in the domain, and no country endings like .co.uk or .com.au. Those tell Google the site serves one country only, and that signal cannot be undone later. Plain .com.

Rules for whichever you pick

  • .com only. Your buyer is a roofer or an estate agent, not a developer — .dev reads as "not a real company" to them.
  • No industry word, no country word. Both are doors that close behind you.
  • No hyphens, no numbers. They get lost the moment someone says the name aloud.

  • Buy 3+ years up front. Cheap, and it removes a renewal risk.

Where the niche actually lives

One site, one page per industry

This is the structure that lets you chase landscapers hard without ever turning away an e-commerce job.

Each page is written as though that trade is the only thing you do: their photos, their words, their case studies, their objections. A landscaper landing on the landscaping page sees a landscaping specialist — the roofing page is not in front of them and never crosses their mind.

Meanwhile every link any of those pages earns feeds the authority of the whole domain — the one thing four separate niche sites could never do for each other.

  • The home page stays broad — "websites and SEO for businesses that need leads" — because it has to catch everything the industry pages do not.
  • Outreach is where you go narrow. Cold emails to landscapers link straight to the landscaping page, never to the home page.
  • Add an industry only once you have a case study for it. A page with no proof converts nobody.

Do not multiply industries by countries

Four industries across five countries is twenty pages, and nobody writes twenty good ones. Google reads the result as thin content and ranks none of them.

Industry pages first; country pages much later. You deliver remotely — a UK landscaper hires you because the page speaks to landscapers, not because the URL contains /uk/. Industry pages carry far stronger buying intent and far weaker competition. Only once one industry is genuinely producing work should you build out its strongest market.

Read this before you spend money

Google Business Profile will not do what you are hoping

The hard limit

You cannot create a Google Business Profile for the USA, UK, Australia or Canada while operating from Pakistan. A profile requires a real, verifiable address in that country — Google verifies it by postcard, phone, or a video call where you show the premises and your signage. There is no remote workaround.

People do try it with virtual offices, mailbox addresses, or a friend's home. Google finds these — and the penalty is not a warning, it is permanent suspension, often taking every other profile linked to the same Google account with it. It is not worth the risk to a business you are trying to build.

So what is GBP actually for?

It is a local tool, not an international one. It puts a business in the map pack — those three results with pins that show for "landscaper near me". It works on one radius around one real address. It was never designed to help a business rank in six countries.

This matters for you in two different ways, so keep them separate:

  • For your own business: create one profile at your real Pakistan address if you want local work. For your international clients, GBP is irrelevant — you will reach them through organic search, referrals, and outreach.
  • As a service you sell: this is the opportunity. Your landscaping clients do have real addresses in their countries, and most have a neglected profile. Optimising it is one of the highest-value things you can do for them — and you can do all of it remotely, because you work on their verified profile, not yours.

GBP optimisation, as a service — what it involves

01

Claim and verify

Client claims the profile at their real address; you get manager access by invite. Never ask for their password.

02

Categories and service area

  • Primary category exactly right — Landscaper, Lawn care service, and Landscape designer are different categories and rank differently.
  • Secondary categories for everything else they do.
  • Service-area businesses hide the street address and list the towns they cover.
03

Complete every field

Hours, service list with prices, description, attributes, booking link. Completeness is a ranking factor and most competitors stop at half.

04

Photos, constantly

Before-and-after shots of real jobs, geotagged, added weekly. Landscaping is visual — this is where the category is won and where most profiles are dead.

05

Reviews, systematically

A request sent the day a job finishes, a short link, and a reply to every single review. Volume, recency and replies all count.

06

Posts and consistency

Weekly posts of finished jobs and seasonal offers. Then make sure the name, address and phone number match exactly everywhere else online — inconsistency is the most common thing holding these profiles back.

The work, in order

Six phases from empty domain to international rankings

The order matters — each phase depends on the one above it. Skipping to content before the architecture is right means rewriting it later.

01

Foundation

  • Buy the .com. Point it at Vercel — the build you already have is Next.js, which is genuinely fast out of the box.
  • HTTPS, one canonical version of the site (pick www or not, redirect the other).
  • Core Web Vitals green on mobile. You are already ahead here; most landscaping-agency sites are WordPress bloat.
  • Organization and Person schema — you have this in lib/schema.ts already.
  • Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verified, sitemap submitted.
02

Architecture, for when countries come later

Build the industry pages first. When you do add countries, there are three ways to structure it and only one is sensible for a team of one:

  • Country domains (site.co.uk, site.com.au) — strongest geo signal, but you are buying, hosting and building authority for six separate sites. Far too much for one person.
  • Subdomains (uk.site.com) — Google often treats them as separate sites, so authority does not flow well. Skip.
  • Subfolders (site.com/uk/) — this is your answer. One domain, one pool of authority, every country benefiting from every link you earn.

Then add hreflang tags so Google shows the right page to the right country. Every page lists every version of itself including itself, plus an x-default for everyone else. Get these reciprocal or they are ignored.

Note: the old country-targeting setting in Search Console was retired. Hreflang and your content are the signals now.

03

Keyword research, per market

This is where most people targeting several countries fail: they research once, in American English, and publish it everywhere. The words genuinely differ — see the table below. A UK landscaper searching "turf laying" will not find a page written about "sod installation".

  • Run research separately per country in your tool of choice; set the location, not just the language.
  • Target the buying phrases, not the vague ones: landscaping website design, SEO for landscapers, landscaper lead generation.
  • Then go one level narrower, where nobody is competing: lawn care website design, hardscaping SEO, garden design website.
04

Content that earns the ranking

  • Money pages — one per service, written for landscapers specifically. Not "web development" but "landscaping websites that book jobs".
  • Country pages — /uk/, /au/, /us/, each in that market's vocabulary, currency, and spelling. These must be genuinely different pages, not one page with the country name swapped, or they are thin content.
  • Proof — case studies with real numbers. "Rankings went from page 4 to position 2, enquiries tripled" beats any amount of description.
  • Helpful articles that your buyer actually searches: how much a landscaping website should cost, how to get more quote requests, why their site gets no traffic.
05

Authority, country by country

Links from a country's own sites are what make Google trust you there. Nothing else substitutes.

  • Landscaping trade publications and blogs — write for them, per country.
  • Trade association and supplier directories in each market.
  • Your own clients' sites: a tasteful credit link in their footer, from a real business in that country. This compounds — it is the most valuable link source you have.
  • Podcasts and YouTube channels aimed at landscapers.
06

Measure and iterate

  • Search Console filtered by country — one market can be climbing while another stalls, and the average hides it.
  • Track enquiries, not rankings. Rankings are a means; booked calls are the goal.
  • Every quarter: refresh the pages sitting at positions 5–15. Moving those is far cheaper than new content.

One decision to make

Portugal and the rest of Europe

Everything above assumes English. Portugal is a different problem: the landscaping businesses there search in Portuguese — paisagismo, jardinagem, criação de sites — and machine-translated pages rank badly and read worse to a native speaker.

My honest advice: leave Portugal until the English markets are working. Reasons:

  • Four English markets (US, UK, AU, CA) are already a large amount of work, and they share one language.
  • A Portuguese section needs a real translator and someone who can answer enquiries in Portuguese. Without that, the leads go nowhere.
  • Ireland is the easy European win — English-speaking, and shares most UK terminology.

When you do add it, it is another subfolder — /pt/ — with hreflang="pt-PT", professionally translated, not auto-generated.
